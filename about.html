Background

I came to computer science (vision, graphics and interactives more specifically) by way of psychology and neuroscience. I studied Psychology and Criminal Justice (with a minor in Computer Science) at Northeastern University, where coursework in cognition, abnormal psychology, neuropsychology, and statistics got me interested in how people perceive and process the world around them, and eventually, in building systems that could do some of that perceiving themselves. That interest pulled me toward VGI, and I'm now pursuing an M.S. in Computer Science at Columbia University, in the Computer Vision, Graphics, Interaction, and Robotics track.

What I work and will work on

Most of my current work sits in 3D reconstruction and rendering from limited input. On the medical imaging side, that means sparse-view CBCT reconstruction and, more recently, recovering multi-basis material images from conventional, single-energy CT scans using Gaussian Splatting. I'm also contributing to a project that streams progressively refined 3D Gaussian splats to AR/VR headsets for remote technical assistance, which comes at a similar problem from a different angle: delivering a good-enough 3D reconstruction quickly, then refining it without disrupting the viewer. The underlying question of how to build a coherent, efficient 3D representation from incomplete or constrained input is one I'm interested in more broadly.

There's a second thread running through my work that I'm still pulling on: attention and cognitive state, and how systems can adapt to them. My research assistant work has touched pupil-EEG coupling and attention-aware BCIs, and I'm contributing to a foveated geometry project that renders 3D meshes at full detail only where a viewer is looking, to cut the rendering load on compute-constrained wearables. Together with my teammates at a hackathon in San Francisco, we attempted to build an EEG-to-text decoder with a comprehension signal built in. This drew my inspiration to build out a gaze-contingent wearable display in the coming months.

My long-term research interest is to build efficient wearable systems that utilize deep learning and computer vision/graphics to adapt to a user's cognitive state and to provide them with the right support at the right time. I believe that understanding gaussian splatting, foveated rendering, and attention-aware wearable systems could be important stepping stones to achieving this goal.
