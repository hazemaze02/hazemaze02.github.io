Grad Research

CBCT Reconstruction via 3D Gaussian Splatting

Columbia University Irving Medical Center, Data Science Institute · under Prof. Yading Yuan

What & why

CT and CBCT scans are the 3D X-ray images used to plan radiotherapy. When a scan has to be split into many partial captures (for example, to track a patient's breathing), each individual reconstruction comes out noisy and low-quality. I'm testing whether a patient's existing, high-quality planning scan can give these noisier reconstructions a head start, using a 3D rendering technique called Gaussian Splatting.

My contribution

Built the full experiment: gathering a public medical imaging dataset, simulating the kind of limited scans this method needs to work with, adding new steps in the current gaussian splatting pipeline, and designing a fair way to measure accuracy that only looks at the patient's body (an earlier version of the evaluation was accidentally counting empty space around the patient, which made results look better than they really were). Confirmed the finding held up by testing it in two independent versions of the reconstruction software.

Outcome

Submitted these results to a medical imaging conference (SPIE Medical Imaging 2026); decision is still pending. Now extending the idea further with my advisor.

Grad Research

Progressive 3D Gaussian Splat Streaming for AR/VR Remote Assistance

Computer Graphics and User Interfaces Lab, Columbia University · under Prof. Steve Feiner

What & why

A project that lets someone wearing an AR/VR headset scan a broken piece of hardware, which a server then turns into a realistic 3D model, so a remote expert can inspect it from any angle instead of watching a flat video call.

My contribution

Working on the app that runs on the headset itself. The server first sends a quick, rough 3D model so the user isn't left waiting, then better versions follow as they're ready. With a teammate, I'm modifying the current gaussian splatting pipeline (which is based on the AnySplat model by Jiang et al., 2025) by experimenting with different ways to mask the object, render finer details, and reconstruct novel objects not seen in the training data.

Current status

Actively in development.

Grad Research

Foveated Geometry for Multiresolution Rendering

Geometry and the City Lab · under Prof. Silvia Sellán

What & why

Our eyes only see sharp detail in the small area we're directly looking at; everything else is naturally blurrier. This project takes advantage of that to make 3D graphics faster: showing full detail only where someone is actually looking, and simplifying everything else, without the viewer noticing a difference. That matters most for devices like AR/VR headsets and wearables, which don't have the processing power to render everything in full detail at once.

My contribution

Wrote the core algorithm that breaks a 3D model into smaller, well-formed voronoi patches. Also built a visual tool the team uses to watch the algorithm work step by step, and wrote the tests that keep it reliable.

Current status

Actively in development.

Hackathon · BCI

NeuroRead

EEG-Based Speech Decoding with Semantic Comprehension Scoring · Neurohackathon, San Francisco, 2025

A non-invasive brain-computer interface that decodes what a person is reading from EEG brain signals and measures how well their brain is understanding each concept, aimed at adaptive, personalized education.

Headline results

The system's best guess was among its top 10 word options two-thirds of the time, far better than random chance (about 1 in 100). It also showed an early, not-yet statistically significant hint of a known brain signal linked to momentary confusion while reading.
