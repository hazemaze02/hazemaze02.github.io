About

Hazel Lim (Hye Min Lim)

Background

I came to computer vision by way of psychology and criminal justice. I studied Psychology and Criminal Justice (with a minor in Computer Science) at Northeastern University, where coursework in cognition, neuropsychology, and statistics got me interested in how people perceive and process the world around them, and eventually, in building systems that could do some of that perceiving themselves. That interest pulled me toward computer vision, and I'm now pursuing an M.S. in Computer Science at Columbia University, in the Computer Vision, Graphics, Interaction, and Robotics track, as a Lustgarten-Whitney Family Fellow.

What I work on

Most of my current work sits in 3D reconstruction from limited visual information. Right now that means sparse-view medical imaging (reconstructing CBCT scans from a fraction of the usual views), but the underlying question of how to build a coherent 3D understanding from incomplete input is one I'm interested in more broadly.

There's a second thread running through my work that I'm still pulling on: attention and cognitive state. My research assistant work has touched pupil-EEG coupling and attention-aware BCIs, and on my own time I've been prototyping a gaze-contingent wearable display and building an EEG-to-text decoder with a comprehension signal built in. I'm not yet ready to claim a single unified thesis connecting sparse-view reconstruction and attention-aware systems. I'm still figuring out where those interests actually converge, but wearables and learning environments are where I keep finding myself drawn back to.

Currently curious about

  • How pupil dynamics and EEG jointly reflect arousal and cognitive engagement
  • What "attention-aware" actually means for a wearable device, and how to build one that behaves accordingly
  • Sparse-view and limited-input 3D reconstruction outside of the medical imaging context
